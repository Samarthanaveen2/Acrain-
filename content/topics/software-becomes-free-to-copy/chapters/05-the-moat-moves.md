By mid-2028, "we built it" is no longer a reason to buy anything. Investors stop asking how hard a product is to build and start asking who already uses it, and why they'd stay.

The jobs shift with it. Writing code is still useful, but it's the cheap part. Knowing *what* to build, getting it in front of people, and being trusted with their data are the expensive parts.

This is the chapter I care about most. If it's right, I should spend the next two years on things that compound: an audience, a reputation for shipping, real users, and an understanding of one physical-world field that software alone can't touch.
