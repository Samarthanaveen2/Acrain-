A year after ArtCraft, most popular paid desktop apps have an open, AI-built alternative that's good enough for students and hobbyists. Professionals mostly stay put, because their work lives in file formats, plugins and team workflows that the clones don't fully match.

Prices start to bend. Subscriptions get cheaper tiers, and companies push harder on the parts a clone can't copy: cloud storage, accounts, stock libraries, AI features that run on their own servers.

Console makers move the same way. New games lean more on online checks and server-side logic, because code that never leaves the server can't be recompiled.

:::expand[What can't be copied]
When anyone can copy what's visible, value moves to what isn't:

- **Distribution:** already having the users
- **Trust and brand:** who you trust with your files, money or health
- **Data:** what improves the more it's used
- **Hardware:** chips, devices, energy, things in the physical world
- **Law:** patents, licences, contracts
:::
