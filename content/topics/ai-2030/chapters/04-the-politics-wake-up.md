AI becomes an election issue. It's the 2028 US presidential race, and for the first time candidates are asked directly what they'll do about job losses, AI-run cyberattacks and the power of a handful of companies.

The public mood sours. People notice that AI is making a few companies extremely rich while making their own jobs less secure. Approval of the leading lab drops, and calls to regulate, tax or even nationalize the frontier labs move from the fringe into the mainstream.

Meanwhile the leading lab's AIs do most of its research. Humans set direction and check results, but the day-to-day work of running experiments is automated. Nobody outside the lab can easily tell how fast things are really moving.

:::expand[Why politics matters for me]
Rules written in 2028 and 2029 decide a lot: who can train big models, whether AI work needs licences, what happens to displaced workers. Paying attention to policy is part of reading the future, not a distraction from it.
:::
