The leading lab's best model writes code better than its best engineers, and it runs as hundreds of thousands of copies at once. AI research itself speeds up, because most of the work in AI research is code.

This is the turn in the story. Progress that used to take a year now takes a few months, and the gap between the leading lab and everyone else grows, because the leader's AIs are helping build the next version.

The capability that worries me most here is biology. Models now match top experts on many lab-science questions. The labs add safeguards, but the same knowledge that speeds up vaccine research could help someone cause harm, and governments start treating bio risk from AI as a security issue rather than a hypothetical one.

:::expand[Signs this chapter is wrong]
- Agent time horizons stop doubling for six months or more
- Labs report that AI-written code needs as much human review as it saves
- No measurable speed-up in how often major models are released
:::
