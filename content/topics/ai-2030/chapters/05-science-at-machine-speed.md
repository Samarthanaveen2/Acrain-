AI research is no longer the only field moving at machine speed. AI systems design experiments in materials, chemistry and medicine faster than labs can run them, and robots start doing more of the running.

The good news is real: new drug candidates, better batteries, faster diagnosis. So is the risk. AI that can plan biology experiments well enough to cure diseases can, in the wrong hands, help design dangerous ones. Biosecurity becomes one of the main things governments argue about, alongside cyber.

The US–China race is now explicit. Both governments see frontier AI as decisive for military and economic power, which makes any slowdown politically hard even as the risks get easier to see.
